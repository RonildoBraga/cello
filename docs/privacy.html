Privacy

Your practice is saved on your iPhone. You choose when to share it.

What the app saves

Cello stores your practice locally: humming audio, cello audio and video, pitch observations, scores, tap timing, lesson progress and a few preferences, such as which introductions you have read. An interrupted or failed save may be kept for recovery.

Pitch detection and scoring run on your device. The app has no accounts, advertising or analytics, and does not connect to a server to store or analyse your practice. It does not automatically upload recordings.

Permissions

Microphone access is used to record sound and measure pitch during practice. Camera access is used only for cello recordings so you can review your hands and bow. Humming uses no camera. Listening to the bundled examples needs neither permission.

You can change these permissions in iPhone Settings.

Sharing a take

Export happens only when you choose it. A take’s ZIP can include the recording, pitch observations, scores and a review summary; takes with video can also include still frames. You choose where the exported file goes in the system share sheet.

Once you share or save an export elsewhere, the receiving app, service or person controls that copy.

Deleting data and backups

Delete takes individually in Recordings, or use Edit → Delete all. Earned lesson progress is retained when recordings are deleted. Deleting Cello removes its local app data; export important takes first.

Your app data may also be included in iCloud or computer backups, depending on your settings. Apple and your backup settings control those copies. Deleting a take or the app does not remove files you already exported or manage existing backups.

Learn about iCloud Backup from Apple.

This website and external links

This support site is hosted on GitHub Pages and includes no added analytics, advertising, forms or embedded media. GitHub may process technical visit information under its Privacy Statement.

Opening a website or GitHub link from Cello takes you to a separate service with its own privacy practices. If you email support, we receive the details and files you choose to send so we can respond. Your email provider handles delivery. GitHub issues are public.
