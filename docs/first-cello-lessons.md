# First cello lessons

The first slice of [issue #6](https://github.com/RonildoBraga/cello/issues/6) is **Meet your cello**: learn the main part names, hear the four standard string pitches and see the route into playing. It can be explored before the instrument arrives. It is an introduction, with no written answers, stars, completion award or effect on existing performance progress.

The diagram is an original app illustration. No source photograph, diagram, method page or new third-party recording is bundled. Playback reuses the existing licensed recorded cello references and their [credits](../CelloPractice/Resources/CELLO-LICENSE.txt).

## Introductory facts and copy

Suggested opening copy: “Learn the parts and hear the four strings, from low to high.” An open string is sounded without a left-hand finger stopping it.

| Part | Short explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Scroll | Decorative carving at the top of the neck. |
| Pegs | Change string tension for tuning. |
| Fingerboard | Where left-hand fingers stop strings to change pitch. |
| Strings | Vibrate to produce pitched sound. |
| Bridge | Supports the strings and carries their vibration into the body. |
| F-holes | Openings in the front that let air move. |
| Tailpiece | Anchors the strings at the lower end. |
| Endpin | Supports the cello on the floor. |

The part names and these functions were checked against [Macquarie University's instrument anatomy](https://www.mq.edu.au/macquarie-archive/lema/cello/parts.html), with labels cross-checked against the [Internet Cello Society's anatomy page](https://www.cello.org/Cello_Introduction/A.html). The university's historical instrument images are copyrighted and are not reused. The short explanations above identify parts; they are not adjustment, tuning or playing procedures.

Standard strings run from low C through G and D to high A. The [Philharmonia orchestra's cello introduction](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/cello/) confirms this tuning in fifths. [Yamaha's instrument introduction](https://hub.europe.yamaha.com/articles/the-essential-cello) explicitly identifies the octave pitches; the [Grinnell College instrument collection](https://omeka-s.grinnell.edu/s/MusicalInstruments/item/654) independently gives the same sequence.

| Low to high | Scientific pitch | Existing app target at A4 = 440 Hz |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C | C2 | 65.41 Hz |
| G | G2 | 98.00 Hz |
| D | D3 | 146.83 Hz |
| A | A3 | 220.00 Hz |

“Low to high” describes pitch order. It is not an unlabelled left/right instruction: diagram orientation and the player's viewpoint differ. Existing references demonstrate pitch and recorded cello timbre; the source is a cello section sample, not a recording of the learner's open-string technique.

Sources checked on 10 November 2026.

## Proposed practical sequence — draft pending review

The future route is **setup → open strings → first notes → Hot Cross Buns phrases → complete melody**. This is a proposed app sequence, not a teacher-reviewed method. [Mel Bay's published beginner-method contents](https://www.melbay.com/Products/21499BCDEB/modern-cello-method-grade-1.aspx) places open-string work, D-string notes and bowing before Hot Cross Buns. We use that as an ordering reference and create our own material; its notation, diagrams and audio are not redistributed.

| Stage | Proposed scope | Evidence needed before claiming completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meet your cello | Parts, standard string names and reference listening. | Factual/source review and accessible UI/playback checks. Reading/listening earns no performance score. |
| Setup | Instrument support, sitting position and bow preparation with individual guidance. | A cello teacher reviews the actual wording, diagrams and the learner's setup. Precise physical instructions remain draft until reviewed. |
| Open strings | Play individual strings with a clear, steady sound; use the existing recording lessons. | Real acoustic recordings and teacher observations of the relevant technique. Preserve the existing lesson IDs and earned scores. |
| First notes | Verified string choices, first-position note targets and fingering for our arrangement. | Teacher-reviewed fingering/notation plus acoustic recordings of the actual targets and transitions. |
| Phrase performance | Play short cello phrases with frozen score/tempo/register and separate pitch/rhythm feedback. | A cello-specific recording route, acoustic assessment tests and independent piece progress. |
| Whole melody | Join the phrases, retaining the complete recording and chart. | A complete acoustic performance under the saved reference; previous foundation, humming or tap stars do not substitute for it. |

The setup and technique stages must distinguish what sound analysis can measure from what a teacher can observe. The app must not infer internal tension, bow pressure, safe posture or technique mastery from pitch confidence or a three-star result.

## Acoustic validation plan for the remaining work

Before issue #6 closes:

1. **Confirm the teaching material.** Review each setup, bowing, first-position, string-choice and fingering instruction with a cello teacher. Record the reviewed version and any learner-specific adaptations. The current introduction alone is not evidence that this review occurred.
2. **Create a cello-specific recording path.** Freeze the actual cello reference before capture; keep voice registers and humming scoring separate. Save the complete audio/video and raw pitch observations through the recoverable session path. Keep reference playback manual and cancel unfinished count-ins when leaving/backgrounding.
3. **Collect labelled acoustic performances.** With permission, record a real acoustic cello playing all four open strings, the melody's actual fingered targets, each phrase and the complete piece. Include repeated notes, quiet attacks, sustained notes, natural articulation gaps, string crossings and harmonic-rich sound. Preserve original takes rather than replacing them with edited reference audio.
4. **Use independent labels.** A teacher or other qualified reviewer identifies sounding pitch, actual starts, audible duration, missed/extra notes and ambiguous segments without consulting the app's grades. Document disagreements and uncertain labels. Physical-technique observations remain separate from the audio rubric.
5. **Check positive and negative cases.** Include clear correct playing, wrong pitch, octave errors, a missing/extra note, wrong string where relevant, early/late starts, shortened holds, background noise, clipping and weak/uncertain sound. The evaluator must not silently octave-correct, shift the performance onto the target or award a trusted score to unclear evidence.
6. **Verify persistence and delivery.** Test frozen score/tempo/version, separate phrase/piece progress, failed-save recovery, reload, playback and export. Confirm that adding cello performances leaves prior recordings and foundation/humming/rhythm achievements intact. Complete real iPhone microphone/camera/output/haptic checks under [issue #16](https://github.com/RonildoBraga/cello/issues/16), preserving the user's account-sign-in deferral until home.

Licensed reference playback, oscillator controls, a Simulator pass and recordings assembled from samples are useful software checks. They do not replace real learner cello performances or teacher review. No new cello performance or setup acceptance item in #6 should be marked complete from this introductory delivery alone.
